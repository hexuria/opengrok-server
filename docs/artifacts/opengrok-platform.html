Part I · What it is

In one page

OpenGrok is a platform for AI coworkers that keep working when you close your laptop. A coworker is a durable, named worker that belongs to a person and an organisation. It holds a conversation, it has its own computer to work on, it reaches your systems through governed integrations, and it can act on a schedule or in response to events without anyone present.

The distinguishing property is where the work lives. In most AI tools the work happens inside a browser tab or a desktop application; close it and the work stops. In OpenGrok the client applications are views. Everything that matters — the conversation, the runtime, the computer, the policy, the credentials — lives on the server. A coworker started from a phone can be watched from a laptop and will finish whether or not either is open.

The second property is that the organisation, not the model, decides what may happen. Every action a coworker attempts passes through policy that the organisation configures: what a worker may do at all, what a given person may make it do, which actions require a human’s explicit approval, and how much it may spend. A model can propose an action. It cannot perform one.

How to read this document. Part I is what the platform is and what it guarantees. Part II is its structure. Part III is the part most organisations care about most — where your data rests, where your boundary sits, and what your administrators control. Part IV is how it operates and what is deliberately outside its scope. Nothing here is a delivery schedule; this is the destination, not the route.

What it does

Six capabilities. Everything else in this document exists to make one of these safe, durable, or accountable.

  • Persistent AI coworkersNamed workers with their own instructions, tools, memory and conversation history. They belong to a person and an organisation, and they outlive any session, device or browser tab.
  • A real computer eachEvery coworker can be given an isolated Linux environment with a shell, a filesystem, a browser and a graphical screen — so it can do work that has file and application state, not only produce text.
  • Governed access to your systemsCoworkers reach your business systems through standards-based integrations with scoped credentials held centrally, rather than through credentials distributed to individual workers.
  • Work on your own machines, with consentA coworker can run a task on a member’s own computer, but only through an application that person installed, only while it is running, and only after that person approves the specific action.
  • Unattended operationCoworkers act on a schedule or in response to events in the system. Unattended work is governed identically to attended work — same permissions, same approvals, same budget, same record.
  • Cost control across your own model accountsAll model usage exits through a single gateway that routes each request to an appropriate model, spends your organisation’s own provider credentials, and records both actual and comparable cost.

What holds true, always

These are architectural properties rather than features: they are consequences of how the system is built, they apply to every client and every entry point, and they cannot be disabled by configuration.

  • Work survives everything a user can do to itClosing an application, losing a network, restarting a device — none of these are events the platform has to handle, because no work is held in a client. Server restarts and instance failures are equally survivable: in-progress work is recorded before each external call and resumed by whichever instance next picks it up.
  • Policy is enforced on every action, not once per sessionPermission is re-evaluated for each individual action a coworker attempts. A session that was authorised an hour ago has no standing authority now.
  • The model never chooses whose data it touchesBefore any action runs, identity arguments are overwritten with the identity of the session that caused it. This is a replacement, not a validation — there is no input the model can produce that reaches another person’s or organisation’s data.
  • Permission layers narrow, never widenWhere several controls apply, the most restrictive answer governs. A misconfigured rule can only reduce access; it cannot grant it.
  • A refusal is an outcome, not a failureWhen an action is declined, the coworker receives that as information and continues working. Denying something does not abandon the task, and does not require a person to restart it.
  • Approval requests do not expireA request for human approval is held on the server and is answerable from any of that person’s devices. Nothing times out into a silent yes or a silent no.
  • Every action is recorded before it happensThe conversation record and the action log are the same durable, append-only history. There is no state in which something occurred and no record of it exists.
  • Provider credentials stay in one placeModel credentials are held by a single component. They are never written to a worker’s configuration, sent to a client, included in a log, or exposed to a model.

Part II · The shape of the system

System map

One deployable platform, a set of client applications that hold no state, isolated execution environments, and clearly bounded connections to the outside. The platform is internally divided into subsystems with strict boundaries — each owns its own data and is reached only through its interface — which is what allows any one of them to be operated separately later without changing the others.

The subsystems, and what each is responsible for

Boundaries are strict by design: a subsystem owns its own data, exposes a defined interface, and learns what happened elsewhere through recorded events rather than by reading another subsystem’s data. This is what makes the platform operable as a single unit today and separable later without redesign.

How a request moves through it

A single instruction — typed by a person or raised by a schedule — crosses the platform repeatedly. The important structural point is where each decision is made, and that the model participates in exactly one of them.

  1. An instruction arrives — typed by a member, or raised by a schedule or an event. The client submits it and holds nothing.
  2. The platform authenticates it and writes it down — the request is attributed to a person and an organisation, and appended to the durable record before anything else happens.
  3. The model is asked what should happen next — through the inference gateway, on your organisation’s own credentials. This is the only step a model takes part in.
  4. Policy decides — the proposed action is checked against capability, review rules, device access and budget. The outcome is allow, request approval, or decline with a reason.
  5. The action runs where it belongs — in the coworker’s execution environment, against a connected business system, or on a member’s own device. Identity is replaced with the session’s before it runs.
  6. The result is recorded — outcome, cost and decision, all against the action that caused them.
  7. The answer is delivered — to every client watching, live. If none is open, it waits in the record.

Part III · Where your organisation sits

Where your data rests

Data is classified by where it is allowed to be stored, not only by who may read it. The classification below is enforced structurally: each class has one resting place, and the paths between them are fixed.

Your organisation’s boundary

The organisation is the unit of isolation. It bounds who can see what, whose credentials are spent, which computers exist, and what the spending limits are. Nothing crosses that boundary without an explicit act by someone inside it.

Sharing within your organisation

Inside the boundary, three things can be shared deliberately: a computer can be shared by several coworkers or reserved to one; an integration connected by one person can be lent to specific coworkers under a rule, without copying the credential; and a conversation can include several coworkers at once. Each of these is a configuration your administrators control, and each narrows rather than widens what a coworker may do.

Where a coworker’s actions can actually happen

There are exactly three destinations. Every action a coworker takes resolves to one of them, and each has a different security posture that your administrators configure independently.

What your administrators control

Governance is configured, not coded. Each control answers a single question and applies to every entry point — the desktop application, the web console, integrations and any third-party client alike.

What is visible for audit

  • Every action attemptedWhat was attempted, by which coworker, on whose behalf, where it ran, and what came back — including actions that were declined and why.
  • Every approvalWhat was asked, who answered, when, and whether the answer created a standing rule.
  • Every model callWhich model, on which credential, how many tokens, actual cost, and comparable cost had a premium model been used throughout.
  • Every integration callWhich connected system, under which connection, on whose authority.

Part IV · Operating it

What runs, and what it needs

The platform is deliberately a single deployable unit with a small dependency surface. The subsystem boundaries described in Part II are internal; they do not translate into separate services to operate.

ComponentWhat it isScaling
The platformOne application, run as several identical instances behind a load balancerAdd instances. Any instance can serve any request and resume any interrupted work.
The databaseA relational database holding the record, the configuration and the credentialsStandard practice for the engine in use. It is the system of record.
Execution environmentsContainer images, one running instance per active coworker environmentThe dominant cost. Idle environments are stopped automatically and retain their storage.
Environment hostingShared infrastructure by default, or infrastructure an organisation nominatesChosen per organisation. Independent of platform scaling.
Model providersYour own accounts, reached through the gatewayMultiple credentials per provider are used in rotation, with automatic recovery from limits.

On deployment location. The platform can run on infrastructure you control, on infrastructure we operate, or split — with execution environments on your own machines and the platform hosted. The boundary drawn in Part III is the same in all three cases; what changes is who operates the hardware.

How it behaves when something fails

Degradation is designed to be partial and specific. The table below is the intended behaviour for each failure, and it is a fair summary of what an operations team should expect.

If this failsWhat stopsWhat continues
A platform instanceNothing. Work in progress is picked up by another instance.Everything.
A member’s device or applicationActions targeting that device.All coworkers, all environments, all scheduled work.
One execution environmentThat coworker’s environment actions, briefly.Other coworkers. The environment is restored with its storage intact.
One model providerNothing, if another credential or model is available.Work continues on an alternative; the failure is recorded.
One connected business systemActions against that system, which are declined with the reason.The coworker continues and can report the problem.
The databaseThe platform. This is the single point of consistency by design.Running environments persist; work resumes when the database returns.

The pattern is that the platform records intent before acting, so an interruption leaves a known state rather than an ambiguous one. Work is resumed rather than restarted, and an action is not repeated merely because a component restarted.

Scope

Stated plainly, because an architecture is judged as much by what it declines to do.

In scope

  • Durable coworkers and their recordConversation, activity and audit history as one append-only record.
  • Isolated executionPer-coworker Linux environments with shell, files, browser and screen.
  • GovernanceCapability, review, approval, device access and spend, enforced per action.
  • Standards-based integrationConnections to business systems through a published tool protocol, with a central credential vault.
  • Unattended operationSchedules and event triggers, governed identically to attended work.
  • Model routing and accountingOne gateway over your own provider accounts, with usage and comparable-cost records.
  • Multiple client surfacesDesktop, web console, and any client speaking the supported agent protocol.
  • Organisational identityOrganisations, invitations, verified domains and roles.

Deliberately not in scope

  • Training or hosting modelsThe platform routes to providers. It does not train, fine-tune or serve model weights.
  • Remote control of a member’s screenNo component can view or drive a personal display. Actions on a personal device are file and command level, and consented individually.
  • Being a system of record for your business dataBusiness data stays in your systems. The platform holds the record of work, not a copy of your operational data.
  • Reselling model accessThe gateway spends your organisation’s own provider credentials for your own members.
  • Unbounded autonomyUnattended work carries the same permissions, approvals and budgets as attended work. There is no mode that removes them.
  • Cross-organisation visibilityCollaboration across organisational boundaries is a defined, explicitly enabled capability — not a default, and not implicit in shared infrastructure.

Terms used in this document

Coworker
A durable AI worker with its own instructions, permissions, history and computer. Belongs to a person and an organisation.
Execution environment
The isolated Linux computer a coworker works on: shell, filesystem, browser and graphical screen.
Action
Anything a coworker does other than produce text — run a command, read or write a file, call a connected system, use a screen.
Integration
A connection to a business system through a published tool protocol, authorised by a scoped credential held in the platform’s vault.
Inference gateway
The single component through which all model requests leave, holding your provider credentials and recording usage.
Approval request
A question put to a named person before a specific action proceeds. Held on the server; answerable from any of that person’s devices; does not expire.
Review rules
Plain-language limits on what coworkers may do, set for the organisation and optionally narrowed per coworker, applied to every action.
Organisation
The unit of isolation: members, coworkers, environments, credentials, policy, records and budgets.

Summary

Durable AI workers, each with a real computer, operating under governance the organisation sets — with the work, the credentials and the record held by the platform rather than by any device.
